Merge-and-sell economy: does it punish impatient picks?

One-page design note for production. Numbers below come from the stated rules; items marked (assumption) are not in the rules.
Verdict: yes, and harder than the "one ninth" claim says. Undoing an impatient pick costs about 2 of ~9 choices (22%): one choice was spent taking it, one more is spent removing it. The slot is then Blank for one fight. If the player never removes it, the cost is 1 choice plus one of eight slots (12.5% of capacity) held by a piece that is not in the build. The 1/9 figure counts only the removal.

Rules in play

  • 8 slots. After each win: take 1 of 3 offered pieces into an empty slot, or remove 1 piece. Never both.
  • 3 identical pieces auto-merge into 1 piece, all numbers x1.5 rounded up. The 2 freed slots become Blanks.
  • Blanks do nothing. A removed piece becomes a Blank. No currency, so no refund and no reroll.
  • About 9 choices per run. Opponent rosters, including merges, are visible before each fight.

Timeline of one impatient pick

Win k: take off-build piece
Fight: slot holds it
Win k+1: remove it (no new piece)
Fight: slot Blank
Win k+2: slot refilled
The slot does no build work from win k to win k+2 at the earliest. Two choices are spent, and one fight is played with a Blank.

Why the cost is more than a fraction

Choices on build pieceResult (3 per merge)Multiplier
9 of 9one tier-3 piece (3 x 3)1.5 x 1.5 = 2.25x, ceilings compound upward
7 of 9 (one impatient pick, removed)two tier-2 + one tier-11.5x, 1.5x, 1x
8 of 9 (pick kept)two tier-2 + two tier-11.5x, 1.5x, 1x, 1x, one slot lost
Merging is a threshold: 9 matching copies are needed for the top tier. Missing even one choice forfeits the top merge, so the loss lands as a lost tier, not as 11% weaker. This assumes the build piece is offered every time (assumption); in practice it will not be, which makes each wasted choice worse.

What softens it

  • The off-build piece may match a later offer and become a second build line (assumption: offers repeat pieces).
  • Merges free slots, so late-run slot pressure is lower: 8 slots hold a 9-copy build because each merge returns 2.
  • Visible opponent rosters let players spot a counter-need early. An "impatient" pick made for a visible threat is a read, not a mistake.

Recommendation

Restate the claim internally as "about 2/9 of the run's choices, plus a Blank fight." If that is too harsh, the cheapest softener is letting removal and a take share one reward when the removed piece was taken on the previous win. Do not add currency; the lack of it is what makes the penalty legible.
