An impatient pick costs one of nine choices to undo, and the pick itself cost another

1. The Blank that waits

Removing X on win 4 leaves slot 3 Blank. It stays Blank through fight 4 and is filled by win 5's pick, the next reward. It cannot be refilled sooner, because win 4's action was the removal.

Cost in slots: one slot for one fight. Cost in choices: the removal.

2. What the picture says

The patient run ends with three merged pieces. The impatient run ends with two merged pieces and a lone C, one copy short of C+, even after paying to undo.

Undoing is not free, and it is not the whole bill.

3. The claim, checked

Holds: the undo is one removal, one choice of about nine (1/9, 11%). The slot is Blank until the next reward.

Understated: the wasted pick is also a choice. Take and undo together is 2/9 (22%).

Cheaper alternative: leave X in. The cost is the wasted pick (1/9) plus one slot held for the rest of the run, which only matters if the roster is crowded.

4. Why the slot rarely hurts

Eight slots, nine choices. A merge turns three copies into one piece and two Blanks, so a focused build uses few slots. The patient run above holds three pieces and has five Blanks at the end. Slot pressure only appears when the build is spread over many different pieces.

The Blank costs power only if X would have done something useful in those fights.

5. Where the real price sits

Merged tiers cost copies. Tier 2 is 3 choices (a third of the run). Tier 3 is 9 choices, the whole run, and leaves no room for a single removal or stray pick. Every detour lands hardest on the deepest build.

6. What the design does not say

The brief fixes "about nine" choices. At 8 choices an undo is 12.5%, at 10 it is 10%. The brief does not say what happens to a merge if copies sit in different slots after a removal, or whether an empty slot at a fight changes the fight. Visible opponent rosters let a player spot a poor fit before taking it, so impatience is a decision, not an accident.
