Merge-and-sell economy: does it punish impatient picks?

Design note for production. Rule set: 8 slots, about 9 choices per run, one action per win (take one of three pieces, or remove one piece). Three copies merge to one piece at x1.5 rounded up, freeing two slots as Blanks. No currency.

Short answer: yes, it punishes, but the claim understates it and names the wrong mechanism. Undoing a stray pick does cost one ninth of the run (the removal turn). The pick itself already cost another ninth, so the true price is two ninths, or one ninth if you never undo it and carry a dead slot instead. The Blank slot is a smaller cost than claimed (one fight, and no extra choice). The larger cost is that merges need whole triplets, so one wasted choice in a tight run can cost a full merge.

2. Checking the claim

ClaimHolds?
Undo costs about 1/9 of choicesYes. Removal is a whole turn because take and remove are exclusive.
Total cost of the mistake2/9 if undone (pick + removal); 1/9 plus a dead slot if kept.
Slot sits Blank until next rewardYes, but the refill is the choice you would make anyway, so the Blank costs one fight with a missing piece, not an extra choice.
Is removal even needed?Only when all 8 slots are full. Merges free two slots each, so slot pressure is often low and keeping the piece is cheaper.

3. Why the real cost is a cliff

  • Merges consume copies in threes. Nine choices fit exactly three triplets, so any waste of 1 to 3 choices loses one whole merge, not a fraction of one.
  • A second-tier merge needs nine copies of one piece. One off-build pick, kept or undone, makes that impossible in a 9-choice run.
  • Rounding favors small numbers lightly and large ones heavily: 2 to 3, 3 to 5, 5 to 8, 7 to 11. Two merge tiers on a base 4 give 6 then 9.

4. What softens it

  • Opponent rosters, including merges, are visible before each fight. A "sounds good" pick can be checked against what is coming, which supports patience and also lets a counter pick be deliberate rather than impatient.
  • Offers show three pieces, so a build copy often appears; a miss costs only the turn spent waiting for one.
  • An off-build piece still fights. If it is strong enough it is not a total loss, only an inefficient one.
  • No currency means no second penalty: the mistake never costs gold, only turns.

5. What I could not confirm

  • Offer odds. How often a build piece appears among the three. If rarely, even disciplined runs waste turns and the impatient pick costs less than shown.
  • Run length. "About nine" matters: at 10 or 12 choices the cliff moves and a stray pick may be absorbed.
  • Losses. Choices come after wins; whether a loss ends the run or only skips a reward changes the 9.
  • Blank vs empty. I assumed a Blank counts as an empty slot you can take into.
  • Off-build value. No data on how strong a stray piece is in a fight.

6. Decisions for the producer

QuestionRecommendation
Is the economy punishing enough?Yes in a 9-choice run. It is punishing through merge math, not through slots.
Is it too punishing?Playtest. If the stray pick feels fatal, add one spare choice (10) or let a removal also take a piece in the same turn once per run.
Fix the wording of the claimSay "one ninth to undo, two ninths with the pick itself; one ninth if kept."
Measure nextSimulate offer odds and run length; track how often players remove versus keep a stray piece.
