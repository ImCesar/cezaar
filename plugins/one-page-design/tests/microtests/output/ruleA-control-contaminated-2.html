An impatient pick costs 2 of 9 choices, and only 1 of them is the undo

The claim holds, with one correction. Undoing the pick takes one choice, about one ninth of a run. The pick itself also took a choice, so the whole mistake is two ninths. The Blank slot is the small cost; the lost choices are the large one.

1. Choice 3: the impatient pick

X sounds good. A would have merged.

Slot 3 takes X. The patient run merges three A into A+ here and frees two slots. The impatient run has none to free.

2. Choice 4: the undo

One choice, nothing else that turn.

Remove X. A removal and a pick never happen together, so the player gets no new piece. This is the one ninth of the claim.

3. Choice 5: the Blank refills

The slot sat Blank for one fight.

The next reward can fill it. That pick is a normal pick, not an extra cost. The impatient run is now 2 choices behind and never catches up.

Taking X spent a choice that would have built the army.
Counted in the 2 of 9, not in the claim's 1 of 9.
Undo
1 of 9 choices, about 11%
Removing X spent a second choice and returned the slot to Blank.
This is the claim's one ninth. At 8 or 10 choices it is 12.5% or 10%.
Blank
1 of 8 slots, for 1 fight
The slot does nothing from the removal until the next reward fills it.
Cheap, because 9 choices and 8 slots leave room, and merges free slots.
What the producer decides. 9 choices and 8 slots leave exactly one spare choice for a mistake, and merging gives back 2 slots per merge, so slots are rarely the limit. In this example the first mistake costs 2 choices and leaves no spare; a second would cost 2 more, which is not tested here. Assumed: new pieces go in the first Blank or empty slot; a merged piece keeps the first copy's slot; a Blank counts as empty for taking a piece; the "about nine" choices is exactly nine; X never merges and has no value. Not tested: offers that never show the build's pieces, and opponents' visible rosters changing the player's pick. Rounding: 1 becomes 2, 3 becomes 5, 4 becomes 6, so small numbers gain most from a merge.
