Impatient picks cost one choice in nine to undo, two in nine in all, and in a nine-choice run that is exactly one merged piece

Verdict for the producer: the economy punishes impatience, but by a flat tax, not a spiral. Nothing compounds, because there is no currency to lose.

The sting is the triple. Nine choices buy exactly three merges (9 / 3), so the run has no slack: waste any one choice and the third merged piece never completes. Undoing the mistake costs one more choice, yet ends in the same place as keeping it. The Blank it leaves is nearly free: slots are not the scarce thing, choices are (never more than 5 of 8 slots in use below).

1The pick

"Z sounds good."

Choice 3 goes to a piece the build will never merge. It is spent whether or not Z is ever removed: 1 of 9, gone.

2The undo

Remove is a choice too, and it replaces the take. Choice 4 builds nothing. This is the "one ninth to undo". Counting the pick, the mistake costs 2 of 9.

3The Blank

The slot is Blank after choice 4 and through one fight. Choice 5 refills it, so it sits idle for one fight only. During it Z was never helping the build anyway, so the real loss is close to nothing.

4The cliff

The patient run ends with three merged pieces and spends all nine choices doing it. Each merged piece is worth three choices, so every lost choice lands on the last piece.

5Keeping it

Leaving Z in the roster wastes only the pick (1 of 9), and it ends with the same two merged pieces as the undone run. Removing pays off only when the board is full or Z hurts, and the run here never gets past 5 of 8 slots.

Assumed here, please confirm

The offer always holds a build piece. A Blank counts as an empty slot you may take into. A merged piece lands in the lowest of the three slots. A kept off-build piece does nothing harmful. Opponents' visible rosters (merged pieces included) let a player see what is worth chasing, which should make Z picks rarer; this page does not price that. Rounding up (x1.5) helps small numbers most: 1 becomes 2, 2 becomes 3, 3 becomes 5.

merged pieces = floor((choices - wasted) / 3); wasted = 1 kept, 2 undone

What to decide

If a flat 2-in-9 tax on a mistake feels right, change nothing. If it feels harsh, lengthen the run to ten or give one free removal. If it feels soft, nothing in this economy punishes keeping dead weight: add a cost to a full board.
