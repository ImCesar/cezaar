A wrong pick costs two of the run's nine choices: one to take it, one to remove it

Eight slots, three offers, one action per win, and no currency. The only price in this economy is a choice.

The claim: the undo is one ninth.

Verdict for the producer

It punishes, but flatly. The undo is 1 of 9 choices, as claimed. The pick it undoes was another, so the full price is 2 of 9 (22%). Keeping the piece costs 1 of 9 (11%).

The Blank is the small cost: one slot of eight for one fight, and a one-piece build never uses more than 4 slots. The real bite is tier 3, which needs all nine choices; one waste ends it.

Question 1 below decides whether any of this hurts.

Not settled by the rules as given; each could change the verdict

  1. Is a merge a gain? Three 4s sum to 12; the merged piece is a 6. If numbers add, merging halves the total and only frees slots. The patience premium rests on this.
  2. Does every offer hold a build piece? Drawn as yes, the best case. If not, patient runs slip too.
  3. What does X do? Drawn as neutral. If it harms, keeping it is worse than shown and the removal earns its choice.
  4. How fast do opponents merge? Their rosters are visible, so a fight-by-fight pace curve would show whether 6, 6, 4 at fight 10 loses.
  5. Starting roster, and may a player decline? Drawn as empty, with no skip.
  6. Merge placement and chaining? Drawn as automatic chains (6, 6, 6 becomes 9), merged piece in the first slot.
  7. Is "about nine" exactly nine? At ten choices, keeping X still reaches tier 3; removing it does not. At eleven both do.
