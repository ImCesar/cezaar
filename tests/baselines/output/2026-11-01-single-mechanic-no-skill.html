Merge-and-sell economy: does it punish impatient picks?

Design note for production. Mechanic: 8 roster slots, one action per win (take one of three pieces into an empty slot, or remove one piece), 3 same pieces merge at x1.5 rounded up, no currency, about 9 choices per run.

Short answer: yes, but the stated claim is incomplete. Undoing an impatient pick costs one choice, which is 1 of 9 (about 11%). The true cost is two choices, 2 of 9 (about 22%), because the original pick also spent a choice and bought nothing. Both numbers apply only when the roster is full. With empty slots the bad piece can just sit there and the cost is near zero.

One impatient pick, choice by choice (full roster)

Choice 5 is not a loss in itself, since you would take a piece there anyway. The lost ground is that the build is two picks behind schedule at every point after choice 2, and the fights at 4 and 5 are played with a worse roster.

When the claim holds and when it does not

SituationCost of the impatient pick
Empty slots remain (early run, or after a merge frees two)About 0. Ignore the piece, keep drafting. Only its slot is lost.
Roster full, piece clearly off-build1 choice to remove plus the 1 already spent: 2/9, about 22%.
Roster full, a copy of it appears laterMay be recovered by merging. Impatience can turn out fine, so the penalty is not guaranteed.
Late run (choices 7 to 9)Removal competes with the last few upgrades. Cost in power is highest here.

What softens or sharpens the penalty

SharpensNo currency means no resale and no buyback. A removal gives nothing back, and the remove and take actions cannot share a win. A merge needs three copies, so every off-build piece also dilutes the odds of completing one.
SoftensOpponent rosters are visible, so a piece can be justified as a counter. That makes "does not belong" a judgement, not a fact. Merges also free two slots at a time, which absorbs a mistake.

Recommendation and what to check

The economy does punish impatience, in tempo rather than currency, at roughly one ninth to undo and two ninths in total. Reword the design claim to say that. The sting depends on slot pressure, so playtest it: log how often the roster is full at choice 4 and 6. If it is rarely full, the penalty is mostly theoretical. Also confirm that "about nine choices" is stable, since the percentages scale directly with it (at 7 choices, one undo is 14%).
