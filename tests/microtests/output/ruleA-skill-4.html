An impatient pick costs one choice in nine to undo, and two counting the pick itself

For the producer

Does the economy punish impatient picks?

Yes, but through the choice, not the slot. A wrong pick costs one choice in nine when it is taken and a second when it is undone.

The claim is right that undoing costs about one ninth. It undercounts the mistake: the pick already cost the first ninth.

Because merges come in threes, the loss is lumpy. In a nine-choice, three-merge build, any wasted choice costs a whole merge, a third of the build.

Because slots rarely fill, players can leave the piece in. That is the cheaper mistake (1 choice, not 2), so the dead-slot rule seldom does the punishing.

If a run offers 8, 9 or 10 choices, one choice is 12.5%, 11% or 10% of it.

1The pick spends a choice

“Sounds good.”

Taking X is the choice for that win. A take and a removal never share one, so X buys no progress on the build.

Cost so far: 1 of ~9 choices.

2The undo spends another

Putting it right.

Removing X is the whole choice for that win, with no take beside it. This is the one-ninth in the claim. Counting the pick, the mistake is two choices.

Cost to undo: 1 of ~9 (11%). Whole mistake: 2 of ~9 (22%).

3The slot sits Blank for a fight

Nothing there to fight with.

The removed X leaves a Blank. It stays Blank through the next fight and is refilled by the next take, which is an ordinary take. The refill is not a third cost.

Dead slot: 1 fight.

4Blanks are normal here

Every good build is full of holes.

Each merge turns three pieces into one and leaves two Blanks. A Blank is not the penalty. The penalty is the lost choice that put a wrong piece there.

Row 1 ends with 5 Blanks of 8.

5The third merge needs all nine

Why one wasted choice bites.

Three merges of three need nine takes. Waste any one choice, kept or undone, and the third merge is gone. Undoing buys back the slot, not the merge.

Assumes the same offers on every path: an illustration, not a simulation.

6The slots rarely bind

Eight slots, nine choices.

The patient row never holds more than 4 pieces. A kept X has room to sit, so undoing is optional and usually not worth a choice. Only a build of nine different pieces runs out of room, and then choice 9 is forced to be a removal.

Peak occupancy, row 1: 4 of 8.

7Opponents are visible

The warning is free.

Each opposing roster, merged pieces included, is shown before the fight. A player can check whether a piece belongs to the build before taking it, so the cost falls on picks that ignored free information.

Not drawn: what the opposing rosters change.
