Merge-and-sell economy: does it punish impatient picks?

For the producer. Rules: 8 slots; after each win take one of 3 offered pieces into an empty slot, or remove one piece, never both; 3 matching pieces merge into one at x1.5 (rounded up) and two slots become Blanks; no currency; about 9 choices per run; opponent rosters visible before each fight.

Verdict: yes, it punishes, but the brief's "one ninth" understates it. Undoing a bad pick costs one choice (1/9, about 11%). The bad pick itself was also a choice that bought nothing, so the full loss is two choices (2/9, about 22%) once the roster is full. If the roster still has empty slots, you can skip the undo and pay only the wasted pick (1/9) plus the slot it blocks.

Why the cost is larger than it looks

  • A merge needs 3 matching picks to get x1.5, so one stray pick delays a merge by a third of its input.
  • Rounding up helps small numbers most (1 becomes 2, 3 becomes 5), so early merges of low-value pieces are strong and a delayed first merge hurts.
  • With no currency there is no way to buy the loss back; the only exchange rate is choices.
  • Remove and take are exclusive, so every cleanup is a turn with no growth.

Why it is smaller than it looks

  • Merges turn 3 pieces into 1 and free 2 slots, so a full roster is rarer than 8 slots and 9 picks suggest. Many players never need the removal.
  • A Blank does nothing, but neither does an off-build piece; the slot is lost either way.
  • Visible opponent rosters let a patient player see what they will face and pick for it, which reduces the temptation to grab what "sounds good".

Assumptions behind the numbers

  • "About nine" choices: at 8 choices one loss is 12.5%, at 10 it is 10%. The 1/9 figure is a midpoint.
  • An off-build piece has no value. If it gives a small real effect, the loss is lower.
  • Only the first fight after each choice is affected by the slot state.
  • Offers are random; the player cannot always find an on-build piece among the three. Forced compromises are not impatience and are not counted here.

What to check before deciding

  • How often does a run reach 8 filled slots before choice 9? If rarely, the removal cost is mostly theoretical and the real cost is 1/9.
  • Do three offers usually contain an on-build piece? If not, the economy punishes bad luck as hard as impatience.
  • Does a Blank slot after removal cost a fight? Compare win rates with 7 and 8 live pieces.

Decision for the producer

Keep the economy if the aim is to make each choice weigh about a tenth to a fifth of the run. If that feels too harsh for new players, the cheapest change is to let a removal and a take share one reward, which would cut the full-roster loss from 2/9 to 1/9.
