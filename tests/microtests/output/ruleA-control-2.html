Merge-and-sell economy: does it punish impatient picks?

Design note for the producer. Eight roster slots, about nine reward choices per run, no currency. Opponent rosters are visible before each fight.

Short answer: yes, moderately, and the one-ninth claim is right but incomplete.

Undoing an impatient pick by removing it costs one choice, about 1/9 (11%) of the run. But the pick itself already spent a choice on the wrong piece, so the full cost of a fully undone mistake is two choices, about 2/9 (22%). If the board is not full, the player can skip the removal and pay only the wasted pick (1/9) plus one dead slot. Nothing refunds either cost, because there is no currency.

The mechanic in one paragraph

After each win the player may take one of three offered pieces into an empty slot, or remove one piece, never both. Three copies of a piece merge into one, with every number multiplied by 1.5 and rounded up. The two freed slots become Blanks, which do nothing. A removed piece also becomes a Blank. A Blank is an empty slot, so the next reward can fill it.

What the numbers say

  • Removal alone: 1/9. The removal choice gives no piece, so the build ends one piece behind for good. This matches the claim.
  • Whole mistake: 2/9. The wrong pick took the slot and the choice that a build piece would have used.
  • Blank duration: one fight. The slot is Blank only between the removal and the next reward. If the removal is the last choice, it stays Blank to the end. Before the removal the slot was no better, since it held a piece that does nothing for the build.
  • Run length matters. 1 choice is 12.5% of 8, 11% of 9, 10% of 10. Two choices are 25%, 22%, 20%.

When the penalty actually bites

  • Slot pressure is the trigger. Eight slots and nine choices means the board only fills if the player takes eight pieces without merging. Removal is forced only then, or when a good offer arrives and no slot is free. Early on, a wrong piece usually costs just the wasted pick.
  • Late picks hurt more. A wrong pick at choice 8 or 9 has no time to be replaced, and a removal at choice 9 is nearly worthless.
  • Merges are expensive. One merged piece takes three choices, so with about nine choices a build holds roughly three merged pieces at most. A wasted choice is a third of one merged piece.
  • Small numbers grow fast. Rounding up gives 1 to 2, 2 to 3, 3 to 5, 4 to 6, 6 to 9, 8 to 12. Small-number pieces gain the most proportionally.

Things that soften or sharpen the penalty

FactorEffect
Visible opponent rosters, including merged piecesSoftens: the player can see what wins, so fewer blind picks. Also sharpens: a visible merged piece makes a flashy single piece look better than it is.
No currencySharpens: no way to buy back, trade, or smooth a mistake. Every correction is paid in choices.
Wrong piece happens to match a later pickSoftens: it becomes a build piece, so it is not wasted. This is the only refund in the system.
Three offers per choiceSoftens: at least one offer is often on-build, so patience is usually available.

Open questions for the producer

  • Can a player take nothing and remove nothing? If so, declining a bad offer costs a choice but keeps the board clean. If not, every offer forces a take or a remove and the penalty is larger.
  • Is "about nine" fixed or varied? A shorter run makes each mistake costlier.
  • What counts as "not in the build"? The 1/9 figure assumes the wrong piece has no value. A mildly useful piece costs less.
  • Does a Blank counting toward the 8 slots matter for offers? We assume a Blank accepts any piece.

Suggested checks and recommendation

  • Playtest or simulate runs with a forced wrong pick at choices 2, 5 and 8, measuring final power against a patient control. We expect the damage to be largest at 8 and smallest at 2.
  • Track how often the board is full when removal becomes necessary. If it is rare, the removal cost is mostly theoretical and the real penalty is the wasted pick.
  • If a 22% penalty is too harsh, the cheapest lever is allowing a take and a free swap on the first choice or two, not adding currency.
  • If the penalty is wanted, keep the rules as written. It is bounded, visible, and recoverable, and it does not end the run.

All figures come from the stated rules, not from playtest data. The 1/9 and 2/9 values assume a 9-choice run and a wrong piece of no value.
