An impatient pick costs a ninth of the run to make and another ninth to undo

One piece, A, is the build. At win 4 the player takes J because it sounds good. Whether J is removed or left in, nine choices can no longer make the top merge.

PatientNine copies of A. Ends as one A++.
Impatient, then undoneSeven copies of A. Ends as A+, A+, A. No A++.
Impatient, left inEight copies of A, plus J. Ends as A+, A+, A, A, J. No A++.

1. Win 3: the first merge

Three A become one A+. The two freed slots go Blank. Merges keep emptying the roster, so slots are rarely the limit.

Across these runs no roster holds more than five of eight pieces.

2. Win 9: the ceiling

Nine A make one A++ (three A+ merging). Every one of the nine choices must be a copy of A. There is no spare.

Stat after two merges: ceil(1.5 x ceil(1.5 x s)).

3. Win 4: the impatient pick

It sounds good. That is the only reason it is here.

J takes a slot, never merges, and uses up the choice an A would have used. First ninth gone.

4. Win 7: the undo

Removing J uses the whole choice, so no A is taken. Second ninth gone. The slot sits Blank until win 8, when the next A fills it.

Six other slots were free, so the Blank cost nothing here.

5. Win 9: leave it in

Skip the undo and the pick costs one ninth, not two. That gives eight copies of A and still no A++. J holds a slot, which is harmless while slots are free.

A roster that is nearly full changes this: see the questions below.

Assumed for the drawing: every offer contains A; a taken piece fills the lowest empty slot; a merged piece lands in the lowest of the three slots it replaces.

Verdict for the producer

It punishes, in choices rather than slots. Undoing J is one choice, so one ninth is right for the undo alone. The pick itself was already one ninth, so a regretted pick costs two ninths. The Blank costs almost nothing until the roster is crowded.

Not settled by this page

  • Is it nine choices or about nine? At eight, A++ is out of reach even for the patient run.
  • Offers are three random pieces. If A is missing from an offer, a non-A take is forced, not impatient.
  • A pick that answers a visible opponent piece is not counted here. Opponent rosters could justify some off-build takes.
  • Builds with four or more distinct pieces fill the roster, where a Blank does cost. That wants its own page.
