Does it punish impatient picks? Yes, in outcome far more than in slots.

In choices: 1 of 9 if you keep the wrong piece, 2 of 9 if you pay to remove it. The brief's one ninth is the removal alone.

In outcome: nine choices is exactly the 3 x 3 copies a top merge needs. One off-build take, kept or removed, closes it.

In slots: almost nothing. A focused build never fills the roster, so the Blank is rarely felt.

1The claim, checked

Taking X spends a choice: 1/9, 11%. Removing it spends another. Together 2/9, 22%. Only the second is the brief's "one ninth".

At 8 choices the pair costs 25%, at 10 it costs 20%. Run length is "about nine".

2The slot sits Blank for one fight

Remove is a whole choice, so the next fight is played one piece short. The choice after it can refill the slot. That refill is a build take you would make anyway, so it adds no choice cost.

How much the short fight hurts depends on piece strength. The rules alone do not say.

3The top merge needs all nine

Three copies make A+. Three A+ make A++. That is 9 copies in 9 choices. Lane 1 ends on one A++. Lanes 2 and 3 end with two A+ and loose pieces.

Assumes the build piece is offered every time. The spec does not say it is.

4Slots rarely bind

Of 8 slots, lane 1 never holds more than 4 pieces and lane 3 never more than 5. Removal only pays when the roster is full or X does harm. Otherwise leaving it costs 1 choice and undoing it costs 2.

Scattered builds fill faster: eight different takes with no merge fill all 8 slots.

6Opponents are visible

Ask the designer before shipping

Does an off-build piece act in fights, help or hinder? It moves the cost of leaving it. Do Blanks count as empty slots for a take? Assumed yes. Does every offer of three include a build piece?
