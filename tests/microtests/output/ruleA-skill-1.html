An impatient pick costs two of nine choices, not one: the take is spent as well as the undo

A run is nine wins and nine choices. Spend one on a piece that is not in the build and the build is two choices late, with the slot occupied, then Blank, before it is filled.

1Where the claim holds, and where it stops

Undoing is one choice: 1 of 9, about 11%. That part is right.

The take was also a choice, so the pick costs 2 of 9, about 22%. The claim names half the price.

Both counts assume the stray is removed the next win.

2The price is lumpy

Merges need three copies, and nine choices only just reach two tiers. Losing two choices costs the whole second tier, not 22% of anything smooth.

Best case shown: every pick is the same piece. Real builds mix pieces, so the cliff is softer but sits in the same place.

3Keeping the stray can be free

With eight slots and nine choices, a stray costs no choice while an empty slot remains. It costs one the moment the roster is full and the build piece has nowhere to go.

So the punishment lands late and all at once, which is when it hurts most.

Check before trusting these numbers

Assumed, not given: a Blank counts as an empty slot; copies of the build piece are offered often enough to reach nine; each win allows exactly one choice; the stray does nothing harmful while it sits. Opponent rosters are visible, so the player had the information to refuse the stray. The cost is legible, not a trap.

If 1/9 is the intended price, nine choices is about right for removal alone; for 2/9 as designed, say so in the tuning notes.
