Merge-and-sell economy: does it punish impatient picks?

Short answer: yes, but the claim undercounts. Undoing a stray pick costs one of about nine choices (1/9, 11%). The stray pick itself spent another choice, so a pick that is taken and then undone costs 2/9 (22%). If you do not undo it, it costs one of eight slots for the rest of the run. Either way, a stray is worth about one third of a merge, since a merge needs three choices.

Rules that drive the result

  • 8 slots. Each win gives one action: take 1 of 3 offered pieces into an empty slot, or remove one piece. Never both.
  • 3 identical pieces merge into 1 with all numbers x1.5, rounded up. The 2 freed slots become Blanks. Removed pieces also become Blanks. Blanks do nothing. No currency.
  • About 9 choices per run. Opponent rosters, including merges, are visible before each fight.

Timing changes the bill

Stray takenEffect
Early (1-3)Cheap to leave: empty slots remain. Removal can wait until slots run short. Hurts via lost merge progress and a weaker fight roster.
Mid (4-6)Board is near full. Removal is forced soon. Full 2/9.
Late (7-9)Worst case. If the removal is choice 9, the Blank is never refilled. A stray also cannot finish a merge in time.

Why slots matter more than the count suggests

  • Nine choices against eight slots means almost no spare room. Only merges free space (each frees 2), so a stray delays the first merge's relief.
  • Merging consumes 3 slots and returns 1 piece, so a stray tends to sit exactly where the next merge needed space.
  • Rounding up (x1.5, ceil) favors small numbers: 1 becomes 2 (+100%), 10 becomes 15 (+50%). Low-number strays gain little from being merged later.

What softens the punishment

  • Visible opponent rosters let the player pick for the next fight instead of for "sounds good". The information exists, so the punishment is avoidable.
  • A stray that happens to counter an upcoming opponent is not a loss. The claim only holds for pieces that fit neither the build nor the matchup.
  • Three offers per win make an on-build piece likely only if offers are not random across a large pool (see open questions).

Verdict and open questions for the producer

Verdict: the economy punishes impatient picks at about 2/9 of the run, harder late. It is a real but moderate penalty, not a run-ending one. Because there is no currency, the penalty cannot be softened by a refund or sale.

  • How large is the piece pool, and how often do the 3 offers include a build piece? If rarely, patience is also punished, since waiting means no pick.
  • Is a no-action option allowed? If not, a player with a full board and a bad offer must remove something good.
  • Do Blanks appear in opponent rosters or count for anything? This is untested here.
  • Playtest: measure win rate by turn of first stray, to check the timing table. The numbers above are derived from the rules, not measured.
