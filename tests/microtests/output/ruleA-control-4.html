Merge-and-sell economy: does it punish impatient picks?

Design note for production. Figures below are derived by arithmetic from the rules as written, not from playtests.

Verdict: yes, but the claim needs one correction. Undoing an impatient pick does cost one choice, about 1/9 (11%) of a run. But the pick itself also spent a choice, so a take-then-undo costs 2 of 9 choices (22%). The alternative is to keep the piece, which costs 0 extra choices but 1 of 8 slots (12.5%) for the rest of the run. The penalty is real and sized about right for a game with no currency. It is weakest early in a run, when empty slots are plentiful, and strongest from about choice 6 on, when the roster is full.

Rules that drive the numbers

  • 8 slots. After each win: take one of 3 offered pieces into an empty slot, or remove one piece. Never both.
  • About 9 choices per run. There is no currency, so choices are the only resource.
  • 3 identical pieces merge into 1 (numbers x1.5, rounded up). The other 2 slots become Blanks. Removal also leaves a Blank.
  • Opponent rosters, merges included, are visible before each fight.

Note on the original claim: "one ninth to undo" counts only the removal. Counting the take as well gives two ninths. In scenario B the take displaced a build pick, which is already one ninth, so the penalty is 1/9 in both B and C before slot effects, and 2/9 only when the piece is later removed.

Where the penalty bites, and where it does not

Bites
  • Roster full (8 filled, reached by choice 8 with no merges). At choice 9 a take is impossible unless a slot is Blank, so removal is forced. A bad pick made earlier turns the last choice into cleanup.
  • Merge builds. A merge needs 3 copies, which is 3 choices. An off-build pick lowers the number of build choices left to finish a merge.
  • Visible opponents. A player who sees a counter-build coming has no spare choice to pivot.
Does not bite
  • Early picks with empty slots left. Keeping the piece costs no extra choice, only the displaced build pick.
  • Picks that turn out to be merge fodder. A second copy of an off-build piece starts a new set.
  • Informed picks. Seeing the next opponent can make a counter-pick correct. That is not impatience, so the game should not punish it the same way.

Merge arithmetic worth a producer's attention

  • Merging 3 copies yields 1.5x of one copy, so it is slot compression, not a power gain. Three copies of a piece become half as strong in total as three unmerged copies, but they free 2 slots. Those slots are only worth something if the player gets a choice to refill them.
  • Rounding up favors small numbers. A stat of 1 becomes 2 (+100%), 2 becomes 3 (+50%), 3 becomes 5 (+67%), 4 becomes 6 (+50%). Low-number pieces gain disproportionately from merging.
  • The two freed Blanks give patient players back flexibility, which makes the penalty for impatience steeper: patient players have more room to recover than impatient ones.

Open questions and what to test

QuestionWhy it matters
Can a merged piece merge again (three merged copies)?Sets the ceiling on slot compression. The rules do not say.
Does an off-build piece only sit idle, or can it hurt (negative effects, bad adjacency)?If it only sits idle, keeping it is cheap and the penalty is mostly the lost pick. If it hurts, removal becomes mandatory.
Is the choice count fixed at 9, or does it vary ("about nine")?The 1/9 figure scales as 1/N. At 7 choices each wasted pick costs 14%.
Are the 3 offered pieces biased toward the player's build?If offers are random, impatience is partly forced and the penalty feels unfair.

Suggested playtest: record per run (1) the choice number of each off-build pick, (2) whether it was kept or removed, (3) roster fullness at that moment, and (4) win count. If scenario C players lose noticeably more than B players, the removal rule is the sharp edge. If A and B are indistinguishable, the economy does not punish impatience enough.

Levers if the penalty is wrong

  • Too harsh: let removal and a take happen together once per run, or add a 10th choice.
  • Too soft: let off-build pieces carry a small penalty, or limit empty slots earlier (fewer slots, or fewer merge refunds).
