Merge-and-sell roster economy: does it punish impatient picks?

Design one-pager for production. Subject: 8 roster slots, 3 offered pieces after each win, about 9 choices per run.

Answer: yes, and the cost is larger than the "one ninth" figure suggests. Undoing a bad pick costs 1 of 9 choices (about 11%). But the bad pick itself also consumed a choice, so the run has spent 2 of 9 (about 22%) to end up where it started, with the slot Blank until a third choice refills it. Whether that hurts depends on slot pressure, which the numbers below show is low early and high late.

Rules in brief

  • After each win: take one of 3 offered pieces into an empty slot, or remove one piece. Never both.
  • Three identical pieces merge automatically into one with all numbers x1.5, rounded up. The two freed slots become Blanks.
  • A Blank does nothing. A removed piece becomes a Blank. No currency, so a choice is the only resource.
  • Opponent rosters, merged pieces included, are visible before each fight.

Where the claim holds and where it bends

ClaimCheck
Undo costs about 1/9 of choicesCorrect for the removal alone: one choice of about nine. Understates total cost by ignoring the wasted pick.
Slot sits Blank until next rewardCorrect, one fight. Not strictly "next reward": a player can use that reward to remove another piece instead, delaying the refill.
Removal is requiredOften not. 9 choices vs. 8 slots, and each merge frees 2 slots, so early on a wrong piece can just sit. Removal is forced only when the roster is full, which is mostly choices 7 to 9.

Why timing changes the penalty

  • Early (choices 1 to 4): slots are plentiful. A junk piece costs one choice, not two, but it can block a merge triple if it was meant to be a copy.
  • Late (choices 7 to 9): roster is full, so junk forces a removal. Few choices remain to refill, so the Blank may stay empty through the end of the run.
  • Merges soften it: a merge frees two slots, so a run with one merge has room to absorb one mistake free of removal.
  • Visible opponents let players see what wins before they pick. That makes impatience a choice, not an accident, which supports the penalty being fair.

Merge arithmetic (x1.5, rounded up)

Stat 1 becomes 2 (+100%), 2 becomes 3, 3 becomes 5, 4 becomes 6, 5 becomes 8. Low numbers gain the most in relative terms. A merge costs 3 choices (3 copies) for one stronger piece plus 2 free slots, so a build chasing a merge commits a third of the run to one piece, which makes any wasted choice relatively more costly.

Producer takeaways

  • The economy punishes impatience: 2/9 choices lost when removal is needed, 1/9 plus a slot when it is not.
  • The penalty is mild early and sharp late. That is a reasonable shape, but it should be intended.
  • The "about 1/9" line in the pitch should read "about 2/9 including the pick itself".

Open questions to test

  • How often is the roster actually full by choice 7? Needs a playtest log or a simulation.
  • Is "about nine" choices fixed or variable? A shorter run raises each choice's weight.
  • Can offers be all off-build? If so, is "remove" a forced waste for patient players too?
  • Does a lone Blank during one fight measurably change win rate? This is untested; the figures above are rule arithmetic, not measured data.

Basis: figures derive only from the rules stated in the brief; no playtest data was used.
